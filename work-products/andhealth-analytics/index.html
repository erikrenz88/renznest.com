Approach

The task required the investigation of the data set to understand any quality issues and to correct them before analyzing and recommending.

  1. A duplicate patient was found and removed from the data set;
  2. A macro to normalize location names for common misspellings and case issues;
  3. Creation of a canonical locations table, correction of appointment and visit tables, as well as lookup table to attribute providers to the appropriate canonical location; and
  4. Creation of metrics tables at a provider and provider-location by day grain.

An additional issue was the presence of South Clinic and Uptown Clinic as home locations for a number of clinicians, but there was no corresponding appointments or visits at those locations. These are included in the dataset to draw attention to a potential data quality or workflow issue.

Metrics

A number of metrics were created for this data set at the provider-location-visit date grain, including:

  • Count of office visits (count of visits with modality of “In-Person”);
  • Count of telehealth visits (count of visits with modality of “Telehealth”);
  • Count of new patient visits (count of visits where “is_first_visit_for_patient” == True);
  • Count of follow up visits (count of visits where “is_first_visit_for_patient” == False);
  • Count of no show appts (count of appointments with status of “no_show” - created in OJS but easily moved to dbt);
  • Count of cancelled appts (count of appointments with status of “cancelled” - created in OJS but easily moved to dbt); and
  • Count of rescheduled appts (count of appointments with status of “rescheduled” - created in OJS but easily moved to dbt).

Additional metrics at a location-visit date grain were created:

  • Completion Percentage (num: count of visit ids with a corresponding appointment id; denom: count of appointment ids);

Reasoning

Metrics were created in a table due to constraints of dbt Cloud licensing and timing. The metric mart could be created in dbt Core but would require Cloud subscription to query the various grains. There are a few methods to consider, including modelling the metrics in dbt Core and creating a data dictionary from dbt to show data and metric lineage and definitions and provide a layer of governance to the organization. dbt Cloud would include the ability to query the dimensions dynamically.

Further, the metrics layer should be structured appropriately with dimensions and facts that are easily traversed by LLMs to effectively respond without token-burn.

Ultimately, these pieces make up the semantic layer - the source of truth for data in the organization. The semantic layer provides a shared, agreed, and governed understanding such that anyone discussing the metrics that impact the organization has a shared understanding about what is impacting the metric and there is no shadow data analysis being conducted to give poor insights, and thereby, poor decisions.

Clinic Utilization Analysis

Ideas and Future Considerations

Dashboards seem to be reviewed and interacted with, but I’ve found they are ignored in business discussion. Instead, I’ve found a report for audiences to read, digest, and interrogate are more productive. The below is an example of reporting I used to provide to business partners to understand issues, provide the overall story and context, and discuss decisions.

If there were more time, I would have investigated additional splits by provider type (specialty) and drilling down into specific signals to rule out possible causes (like the extremes of weekly completion rates or seasonality effects) alongside statistical significance testing.

Clinic utilization can be driven by many things. Case complexity, patient mix, seasonality, and clinician satisfaction are all examples of drivers. But to understand the impacts, a top down approach is best.

Completion rate (number of completed visits over number of appointments) can provide further information on the visit volume by location.

The points on the line charts indicate that week’s completion rate (defined above) by location, capped at 100% (meaning all appointments were completed). Reasons for unutilized appointments included no-shows, cancellations, and reschedules. There was also a small amount of appointments that had no matching visit in the dataset which should be investigated for data quality or workflow improvements.

Patient satisfaction: last quarter’s visits, by payer type

Another area to review for utilization is how satisfied patients are with the care they are receiving. One slice to view this through is by payer type.

There are some inherent assumptions in payer type that should be investigated, such as higher complexity in Medicaid or self-pay populations due to more challenging socio-political determinants of health, or commercial payer experience swaying care perception (i.e. lack of payment leads to bias in care perception).

In any case, splits by payer mix can prove useful for patient populations that may require more attention.
